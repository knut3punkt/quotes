# Quote Interpretation Generation

## Purpose

Generate one or more genuinely distinct, plausible interpretations of a quotation for use in the quotes application.

The feature is manually triggered from the admin application for selected quotes.

Interpretations are generated by the locally hosted LLM using the existing llama-server integration.

This feature is not intended to establish the objectively correct meaning of a quote or the author's actual intention.

---

## Core distinction

An interpretation is not merely a paraphrase.

A paraphrase restates approximately what the quotation says.

An interpretation provides a meaningful way of understanding the quotation by identifying, for example:

* an implication,
* an underlying idea,
* a conceptual framework,
* a symbolic reading,
* a psychological reading,
* an ethical reading,
* an existential reading,
* a metaphysical reading,
* a spiritual reading,
* or another genuinely useful perspective.

Do not require any particular category.

The appropriate interpretive lenses should emerge from the quotation.

---

## Example: genuinely different interpretations

A useful interpretation system must be able to recognize when the same words support substantially different readings rather than merely generating several paraphrases.

Consider John 3:3:

> Except a man be born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God.

### Literal / exoteric interpretation

To be "born again" can be understood as undergoing a religious conversion or spiritual rebirth into Christian faith.

On this reading, a person cannot truly perceive or participate in the kingdom of God while remaining in their previous spiritual condition. A fundamental transformation is required: the person becomes, in a meaningful sense, a new person through faith.

Here, "born again" refers primarily to a religious and spiritual transformation associated with becoming a Christian.

### Spiritual / psychological interpretation

"Born again" can also be understood more broadly as a radical transformation of consciousness.

A person normally experiences reality through accumulated assumptions, habits, biases, identities, fears, desires, and inherited ways of interpreting the world. These structures can constrain what the person is capable of seeing or understanding.

On this reading, being "born again" means becoming sufficiently aware of these structures to question and wrestle with them rather than unconsciously seeing everything through them.

The "kingdom of God" can then represent a level of understanding or awareness that cannot simply be added to one's existing worldview. It requires a transformation of the person doing the seeing.

This transformation may involve:

* recognizing one's own biases and assumptions,
* becoming capable of seeing from perspectives other than one's own,
* confronting unconscious or habitual patterns of thought,
* loosening identification with an existing conception of oneself,
* and developing a broader or more integrated form of awareness.

The claim is therefore not merely that new information is required. The perceiver must change.

---

These interpretations make substantially different claims.

In the literal/exoteric reading, "born again" refers to religious rebirth and entry into Christian spiritual life.

In the spiritual/psychological reading, "born again" refers to a transformation in the structure of perception and consciousness itself.

A good interpretation model should be capable of discovering differences of this kind where the language reasonably permits them.

It should not merely paraphrase:

> "A person must change before they can understand God's kingdom."

That formulation collapses the ambiguity instead of exploring it.

An illustrative structured result might be:

```json
{
  "interpretations": [
    {
      "lens": "Christian / Exoteric",
      "interpretation": "Being 'born again' can be understood as undergoing a spiritual rebirth into Christian faith. The saying presents this transformation as necessary for perceiving or participating in the kingdom of God.",
      "textualSupport": 94,
      "speculativeness": 10
    },
    {
      "lens": "Spiritual / Psychological",
      "interpretation": "Being 'born again' can be read as a radical transformation of consciousness. A person must become aware of and wrestle with the biases, assumptions, identities, and habitual perspectives through which they normally perceive reality before they can understand realities that their previous way of seeing concealed from them.",
      "textualSupport": 67,
      "speculativeness": 58
    }
  ]
}
```

The numerical scores above are illustrative rather than authoritative.

The important behavior is that the model should be able to distinguish between:

* interpretations strongly grounded in conventional or immediate readings of the text,
* and interpretations that make a larger conceptual leap while still maintaining a coherent relationship to the words of the quotation.

Higher `speculativeness` does not mean that an interpretation is inferior. It means that the interpretation requires more conceptual assumptions beyond what is explicitly stated in the quotation.

The model should preserve potentially valuable speculative interpretations while avoiding readings whose relationship to the text becomes arbitrary.

# Interpretation behavior

## 1. Generate distinct readings

Generate approximately 1–4 interpretations where warranted.

This is a target, not a requirement.

A quote with one clear meaningful reading may receive only one interpretation.

A rich or ambiguous quote may receive several.

Do not create additional interpretations simply to reach a target number.

Returning zero interpretations is valid for trivial, purely factual, or otherwise unsuitable text.

---

## 2. Do not force lenses

Do not require output categories such as:

* literal,
* psychological,
* mystical,
* metaphysical,
* political,
* spiritual,
* religious.

A model required to fill every category is likely to manufacture unsupported meanings.

Instead, the model should determine an appropriate short `lens` label for each interpretation.

Examples:

* "Psychological"
* "Existential"
* "Non-dual"
* "Ethical"
* "Social"
* "Epistemological"
* "Stoic"
* "Symbolic"
* "Identity and habit"

The label should describe the interpretation that was actually generated.

It is acceptable for two interpretations to use similar broad lenses if their meanings are genuinely distinct.

---

## 3. Plausibility rather than certainty

An interpretation must be reasonably compatible with the supplied quotation.

It does not need to be the only interpretation.

Different interpretations may:

* emphasize different aspects of the text,
* rely on different conceptual frameworks,
* or even conflict with one another.

Ambiguity is not an error.

Do not collapse genuine ambiguity into a single supposedly authoritative interpretation.

---

## 4. Do not claim authorial intent

Unless explicit supporting context has been provided, do not claim:

* "The author meant..."
* "The author intended..."
* "This proves the author believed..."

The model is interpreting the text.

Preferred conceptual framing is:

* "The quote can be read as..."
* "On this reading..."
* "This interpretation treats..."
* "The passage may suggest..."

The stored interpretation itself does not have to repeat such hedging mechanically in every sentence, but it must not misrepresent speculation as established authorial intent.

---

## 5. Allow deeper and esoteric readings

Interpretations do not have to be limited to the most literal reading.

Symbolic, metaphysical, mystical, psychological, theological, or esoteric interpretations may be valuable when the text reasonably permits them.

However:

* do not create such readings merely because those categories exist,
* do not present them as the only meaning,
* and indicate when they require substantially more interpretive assumptions than the surface reading.

For example, a statement about "light" may sometimes support a symbolic interpretation involving awareness or knowledge.

It should not automatically be interpreted as mystical consciousness simply because the word "light" occurs.

---

## 6. Preserve the distinction between textual support and speculation

Each interpretation should have a `textualSupport` score from 0–100.

This represents:

> How strongly can this reading reasonably be grounded in the words and structure of the supplied quotation?

It does not represent whether the interpretation is philosophically or factually true.

Each interpretation should also have a `speculativeness` score from 0–100.

This represents:

> How much additional conceptual framing or assumption is required beyond the explicit surface content?

Examples:

A close ethical reading might have:

* textualSupport: 94
* speculativeness: 12

A plausible mystical interpretation might have:

* textualSupport: 68
* speculativeness: 71

A speculative interpretation is not automatically bad.

The scores are descriptive metadata that allow the application to distinguish close readings from adventurous ones.

---

## 7. Interpretation quality

Each interpretation should:

* say something substantive,
* be understandable without reading the other generated interpretations,
* explain a meaningful reading rather than merely assign a category,
* avoid unnecessary verbosity,
* and normally be substantially shorter than an essay.

Suggested initial size:

Approximately 1–3 sentences.

Prefer clarity and density over length.

---

## 8. Distinctness

Interpretations from the same quote should differ semantically, not merely stylistically.

Reject results where:

* two interpretations make essentially the same claim using different wording,
* one interpretation is merely a more verbose version of another,
* different lens labels disguise the same meaning.

Example of insufficient distinction:

A:

> The quote suggests that habits shape identity.

B:

> Psychologically, repeated habits determine the kind of person someone becomes.

These should normally be considered the same interpretation.

---

# Context supplied to the model

## Required

Always provide:

* quote text.

## Optional metadata

When already available and relevant, the request may also provide:

* author,
* work/source,
* source type,
* surrounding context,
* original longer quote when the current quote is an extracted excerpt.

Metadata should be explicitly distinguished from quotation text.

The model must not invent missing biographical or historical facts.

---

# Extracted quotations

If an interpretation is being generated for an excerpt created by the quote-extraction feature, consider supplying the original parent quotation as context.

For example:

QUOTE TO INTERPRET:

"..."

ORIGINAL CONTEXT:

"..."

The model should interpret the selected quote while using the original context to avoid readings contradicted by the surrounding passage.

The interpretation should still describe the selected quote rather than the entire parent passage.

This can significantly reduce context-stripping errors.

---

# Model output

Suggested conceptual representation:

```json
{
  "interpretations": [
    {
      "lens": "Psychological",
      "interpretation": "The passage can be understood as describing how repeated patterns of action gradually form a person's identity rather than merely expressing an already fixed self.",
      "textualSupport": 92,
      "speculativeness": 18
    },
    {
      "lens": "Existential",
      "interpretation": "The quote can also be read as rejecting a fixed essence of the self: who a person is emerges through what they continually choose and enact.",
      "textualSupport": 78,
      "speculativeness": 42
    }
  ]
}
```

# Structured response schema

The production llama-server request should constrain the response structure where supported.

Conceptually:

* root object
* `interpretations`: array
* minimum 0
* maximum 4

Each interpretation:

* `lens`: non-empty short string
* `interpretation`: non-empty string
* `textualSupport`: integer 0–100
* `speculativeness`: integer 0–100

Do not rely exclusively on schema enforcement.

Deserialize and validate the response in application code.

---

# Application-side validation

Validate at minimum:

* maximum interpretation count,
* non-empty lens,
* reasonable maximum lens length,
* non-empty interpretation,
* reasonable maximum interpretation length,
* score ranges,
* duplicate normalized interpretation text.

Malformed individual results should not invalidate otherwise valid interpretations where recovery is straightforward.

Do not automatically trust model-generated scores.

They are useful ranking and diagnostic signals.

---

# Semantic duplicate handling

Initial implementation may rely on the model prompt to avoid duplicate interpretations.

Do not add an embedding dependency initially unless evaluation demonstrates a real duplicate problem.

A later implementation may compare generated interpretations using embeddings and suppress highly similar results.

Design persistence so this can be added without changing the conceptual interpretation model.

---

# Existing interpretations

When the admin user requests interpretation generation for a quote that already has interpretations, the behavior must be explicit.

Preferred initial behavior:

* do not silently delete existing interpretations,
* generate a new candidate set,
* let the existing application architecture determine whether candidates replace, append to, or are reviewed against current values.

The implementation plan should inspect current admin and persistence patterns before deciding this behavior.

---

# Runtime system prompt — version 1

You generate distinct plausible interpretations of quotations.

Your task is interpretation, not merely paraphrasing.

An interpretation should add a meaningful way of understanding the quotation: for example an implication, underlying concept, philosophical reading, psychological reading, symbolic reading, ethical reading, metaphysical reading, spiritual reading, or another useful perspective.

Do not force any particular interpretive lens. Choose lenses that genuinely fit the quotation.

Generate only genuinely distinct interpretations. Two differently worded versions of the same basic meaning count as one interpretation.

Interpretations may disagree with one another when the quotation genuinely permits different readings.

Do not claim to know the author's intention unless explicit context establishes it. Interpret the supplied text rather than inventing facts about the author.

Both close and more speculative readings may be useful.

For each interpretation:

* `textualSupport` measures how strongly the reading is grounded in the supplied text.
* `speculativeness` measures how much additional conceptual framing or assumption the reading requires.

A speculative interpretation is not automatically invalid, but it must remain reasonably compatible with the quotation.

Do not manufacture mystical, metaphysical, religious, psychological, political, or other meanings merely to create variety.

Prefer depth over quantity.

A paraphrase that merely restates the quotation is not a useful separate interpretation.

If the quotation does not support meaningful interpretation, returning no interpretations is valid.

If original surrounding context is supplied, use it to prevent interpretations that are contradicted by that context.

Return only the structured response required by the response schema.

---

# Suggested generation settings

Interpretation generation benefits from somewhat more diversity than deterministic quote extraction.

Suggested initial values:

* temperature: approximately 0.4–0.6
* streaming: false
* sufficient output token budget for up to four short interpretations

Do not tune these aggressively until an evaluation corpus exists.

The exact values should be configurable.

---

# Persistence

Interpretations should be associated with their quote.

Potential metadata worth retaining:

* quote ID,
* interpretation text,
* lens,
* textualSupport,
* speculativeness,
* generation model identifier,
* prompt version,
* generation timestamp if useful.

Whether all generation metadata belongs in the primary interpretation table or separate provenance data should follow existing project conventions.

Do not store model chain-of-thought or hidden reasoning.

---

# Manual admin workflow

Generation is manually triggered for selected quotes.

The operation should be appropriate for bulk selection.

Requirements:

* processing one failed quote should not necessarily fail the entire selection,
* show useful progress/status consistent with existing admin patterns,
* preserve errors at the individual quote level where useful,
* avoid accidental repeated generation caused by UI double submission,
* do not silently overwrite human-edited interpretations.

The plan should inspect the existing quote-extraction bulk workflow and reuse it wherever appropriate.

---

# Failure behavior

Interpretation generation is enrichment and must not endanger quote data.

Failures may include:

* llama-server unavailable,
* timeout,
* invalid structured response,
* empty generation,
* validation failure.

A failure must not delete or modify the original quotation.

Bulk processing should report partial success when appropriate.

---

# Evaluation corpus

Create a small manually reviewed interpretation evaluation set.

Include different kinds of quotations:

* straightforward philosophical aphorism,
* highly ambiguous philosophical statement,
* metaphor,
* religious/scriptural passage,
* mystical language,
* psychological observation,
* scientific or rationalist quote,
* humorous quote,
* political/social observation,
* quote whose obvious reading is sufficient,
* quote that strongly permits opposing interpretations,
* trivial quote where no deeper interpretation is warranted,
* extracted quote whose parent context rules out an otherwise tempting reading.

For each test quote, human evaluation should consider:

### Interpretive value

Does the interpretation add something beyond paraphrase?

### Textual support

Can the reading reasonably be derived from the quotation?

### Distinctness

Are the generated interpretations genuinely different?

### Insight

Does the interpretation illuminate something useful about the quote?

### Overreach

Does it import concepts that the quotation does not reasonably support?

Do not treat exact wording as the expected output.

This is an evaluation corpus, not a deterministic unit-test fixture.

---

# Deterministic tests

Normal automated tests should cover:

* request construction,
* optional contextual fields,
* response deserialization,
* validation,
* malformed responses,
* score boundaries,
* duplicate exact outputs,
* persistence behavior,
* bulk partial failure behavior,
* prompt-resource loading,
* configuration,
* model service failure.

Ordinary unit tests must not require a running llama-server.

Integration tests against the real local model should remain separately runnable.

---

# Future possibilities

Do not implement these as part of the initial feature unless evaluation justifies them:

* embeddings for interpretation deduplication,
* second-pass critic model,
* user-selected interpretive lenses,
* documented historical interpretations retrieved from external sources,
* interpretation-to-tag generation,
* interpretation embeddings,
* automatic interpretation generation during quote import,
* interpretation quality voting,
* model comparison experiments.

In particular, distinguish future "documented interpretations" from generated plausible interpretations.

If we later want to state how a real philosopher, religion, school, commentator, or scholarly tradition has actually interpreted a text, that should use retrieved evidence rather than asking the model to invent such attribution.
