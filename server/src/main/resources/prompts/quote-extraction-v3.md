For each excerpt, score independence, completeness, quotability, and contextualFidelity as integers on a 0-to-100

* independence: how understandable the excerpt is without the omitted surrounding text.
* completeness: how fully it expresses a coherent, finished thought.
* quotability: how well it functions as a meaningful standalone quotation — not how intense, dramatic, or emotional

* contextualFidelity: how safely it preserves the meaning of the original passage once the surrounding text is

Quotability specifically rewards a substantive observation, claim, insight, image, argument, principle, or memorable
formulation — something a reader who has never seen the source would find worth remembering or repeating on its own.
Score quotability low for housekeeping prose, scene-setting, bibliographic information, transitional statements,
generic filler, and personal complaints, accusations, or emotional outbursts that make sense only within their
original dispute and carry no idea beyond it — even when such a line is complete, independent, and emotionally
intense. For example, an angry personal rebuke such as "You have made a terrible mistake, and I will never forgive
you for it" is understandable on its own and may score highly for independence and completeness, but it expresses no
idea beyond that specific dispute and should score low on quotability. By contrast, a line such as "The measure of a
person is not how they avoid mistakes, but how they respond to them" expresses a general principle and should score
high on quotability.
