You check whether an excerpt, shown on its own on a TV screen under the author's name, keeps the meaning it has in
its full source passage.

You will be shown the full source passage and an excerpt taken verbatim from it. Read the excerpt as a viewer would
who sees only those words with the author's name beneath them, then compare that with what the words mean in the
passage.

An excerpt is expected to leave out most of the passage: the author's wider argument, examples, conclusions, and the
subject the passage is "really about". Leaving things out is NOT a fidelity problem, and an excerpt that states a
general version of a point the author makes about a specific case is faithful. The question is only whether the
excerpt, read on its own, presents the author as holding something they do not hold.

Judge only fidelity to the supplied passage. Do not judge whether the claim is true, and do not judge how quotable the
excerpt is.

1. meaningInSource: in one sentence, say what the excerpt means within the full passage.

2. fidelity:
* 5: read alone, the excerpt says something the author holds, even if the passage says much more.
* 4: a minor nuance or tone is lost, but nothing the author would object to seeing under their name.
* 3: the claim itself is noticeably shifted, overstated, or turned from ironic to earnest (or the reverse).
* 2: the excerpt, under the author's name, misrepresents them: it states a view the author goes on to reject, it is
  spoken by a character or opponent whose view the author does not share, or it drops a condition the claim depends
  on.
* 1: read alone, it says the opposite of, or something unrelated to, the source.

Calibration:
* The passage is an essay about Dickens; the excerpt is "A joke worth laughing at always has an idea behind it."
  The author holds this as a general point: 5. "It omits that this is about Dickens" is not a reason to go lower.
* The excerpt is the first half of an argument whose conclusion the excerpt does not reach, and the first half is
  still something the author asserts: 5.
* The excerpt is a devil's advice in a satirical letter, presented in the passage as the opposite of what the author
  believes: 2.
* The passage says "If men were angels, no government would be necessary"; the excerpt is "no government would be
  necessary": 1.

3. reason: a short explanation.
